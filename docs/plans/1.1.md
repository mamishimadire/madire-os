### D2. The longest console pause from the run-time exchange, and the idle loop

- **The wait:** at most 2 replies per exchange (0xED, the state), plus at most 3 resends per byte on 0xFE. Each reply is waited for with `cli` / check / `sti; hlt`, so a reply that has already arrived is never slept past. Every wait is capped at 3 ticks (≤ 30 ms), and **a timeout ends the exchange and turns the lights off for the rest of the boot**.
- **Longest pause:** normal exchange = two reply times (QEMU and VirtualBox emulate the reply as soon as the byte is written, well under 1 ms; a PS/2 keyboard takes about 1-2 ms per byte each way, under 10 ms for both). Worst case, once per boot: 7 replies plus one 30 ms timeout, **under 50 ms**. Exchanges happen only when a lock key changes a light, never on other keys.
- **Nothing in the idle loop is starved:** interrupts stay on throughout, so IRQ0 (ticks and preemption) and IRQ1 (keys queued, 64 events; the fastest test pace, 30 ms, queues at most 4 events in 50 ms) keep running. Other threads still get their turns: a timer preemption can switch away from thread 0 during the `hlt` as at any point of the idle loop. Thread 0's own duties wait for at most the pause above: `console_poll` (sleep ends, programs collected, one `cat`/`cp` chunk), the program output queue (writers yield meanwhile, as when the queue is full), the dropped-key and unexpected-IRQ reports, the `uptime: N s` heartbeat (late by at most the pause, never skipped: it compares whole seconds) and `thread_idle`. The exchange is never run with preemption disabled or from interrupt context. The diagnostics (`test=keyboard`, `test=kbdflood`) read keys in their own loops and never sync the lights (the lights stay as set at boot).

## R. Risks (part 2)

1. **The light exchange on other controllers (medium):** a keyboard or firmware (USB legacy emulation) that mishandles 0xED. Every wait is bounded; one failure turns the lights off until restart; the keyboard's status never depends on the lights.
2. **Keys lost or doubled around an exchange (low):** the IRQ handler keeps queuing keys during the wait; L5, C8 and the VirtualBox `Qr` rows check the typed text.
3. **No trace backend on Actions (medium, unknown):** the stage 5 probe decides; n = 0 is a STOP before stage 7.
4. **VirtualBox GUI (low, untested):** the VirtualBox window may press Caps Lock or Num Lock in the guest to match the host's lights. The acceptance runs headless and is unaffected; the owner's manual check may show it.
5. **Laptops with an embedded keypad (out of scope):** with Num Lock on, some old laptops turn letter keys into digits; real hardware is out of scope (virtual machines only), and it is the reason Linux starts with Num Lock off.
6. **The 20 ms hold (low):** it changes the timing of the sessions in section L; the stage 5 gate (same counts, all passing) catches a change. `test=kbdflood` now holds as long as its gap (20 ms); its checks are conservation-based.
7. **Ctrl+C reaching the prompt late in existing sessions (low):** now visible (`^C`, a log line), but only where the command's own check already fails.
8. **Stack and size (low):** the completion buffers are static; the figures of section O are reported at every stage.
9. **The key-logging rule:** no new path logs typed text; cleared lines are never logged (C3 and the VirtualBox `Absent` row prove it); light changes only with `keylog`.
